01About the competition

A platform for those who aim to strengthen health systems with artificial intelligence

Applicants may apply as a team or as individuals. This year marks the 8th global HSIL Hackathon and the 2nd held in Thailand, organized by Mahidol University.

Who can apply

Students Physicians and health professionals Researchers Engineers Designers Policymakers Entrepreneurs Faculty members

Basic criteria

  1. 01

    Teams of 3–5

    Teams with members from a range of disciplines are encouraged. Individual applicants may join the team-matching session arranged by the organizers.

  2. 02

    No prior idea required

    A registered company or prototype is not required. Teams develop their work from real health system challenges on the day of the competition.

  3. 03

    Free, and the work belongs to the team

    There is no application fee, no equity is taken, and HSIL claims no rights to the team's intellectual property.

  4. 04

    Held simultaneously worldwide

    Every Hub runs the competition in the same period. Winning teams go on to Harvard's continuing program.

06FAQ

Further information for applicants

Is there an application fee?

There is no application or program fee, and no equity is taken. Participants cover their own travel and accommodation during the competition in Bangkok.

What are the eligibility requirements and minimum age?

School and university students, health professionals, researchers, engineers, designers, entrepreneurs and faculty members may apply. Applicants must be at least 15 years old on the day of the competition.

What do applicants under 18 need to do?

Applicants must attach a signed parental consent form when applying. The form can be downloaded from the application page, and a teacher or parent must be reachable throughout the competition.

Do applicants need a team or an idea beforehand?

No. Applicants may apply individually or as a team; teams of 3–5 members from a range of disciplines are recommended. A registered company or an idea prepared in advance is not required.

Do applicants need programming skills?

No. Strong teams usually combine members from health, technology and business. What matters is explaining clearly what problem the work solves, how, and for whom. Teams may present with a mock-up or prototype.

Which languages are used in the competition and the pitch?

Thai may be used during the competition. The final pitch may be given in Thai or English, but slides must be in English, because winning teams work in English in Harvard's Bootcamp.

Who owns the work?

The work belongs to the team, and Harvard HSIL claims no intellectual property rights. Teams must follow the Thailand Hub's intellectual property policy.

Do winning teams in Thailand go straight to Boston?

Not straight away. Teams that win at Hub level join Harvard's Bootcamp, where teams are selected in rounds. Only finalist teams pitch at Demo Day in Boston; they cover their own travel, and HSIL issues letters of invitation.

Will participants receive a certificate?

Participants who attend both days receive an e-certificate from the Thailand Hub by email within two weeks of the competition.

How and when can applicants apply?

Applications are made through the Thailand Hub from December 2026 to mid-February 2027. Once applications open, the apply button will appear at the top of this page.
